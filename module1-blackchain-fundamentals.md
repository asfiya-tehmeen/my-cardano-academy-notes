Blockchain - trust broker between different parties that cannot or will not trust each other

Blockchain technology acts as a decentralized trust broker, using a distributed digital ledger to eliminate intermediaries and facilitate trustless, permissionless global asset exchange. 

With blockchain, trust is placed in a decentralized protocol. You will always here the terms - trustless and permissionless.

A blockchain is a distributed ledger transactions. Meaning?
![[Pasted image 20261013212334.png]]
- When people use digital currency, they are exchanging ownership of digital asset.
- The transaction is locked on a blockchain and not stored in one central location, but rather on many different nodes all around the world. Each node has its own copy of blockchain.
- So when a new transaction is executed, each node updates its copy
- Anyone with an internet connection can exchange assets across continents

Trustless transactions are enabled between parties with all past transactions logged and verified on the blockchain. 
There is no need of an intermediary such as a bank to verify transactions.
Transactions are immutable and transparent, secured by network consensus, making them highly resilient. 
*meaning the transactions can't be altered or reversed without updating the entire chain and updates need the consensus of most of the network *

A dishonest actor need to have 51% control of the network to be successful. The cost of acquiring such control is so expensive, it is a deterrent in itself.

Blockchain transactions are transparent and auditable as all transactions are a public single source of truth.

it is completely resilient, as even if some of its peers go offline, the rest will continue functioning.
![[Pasted image 20261013213936.png]]

The immutability and security of a blockchain is directly related to its decentralization.
the blockchain trilemma, which involves a trade-off between decentralization, security, and scalability, with blockchains typically excelling in two of these areas. 

Different blockchain types are covered: public, private, and federated. 

Measuring decentralization is not straightforward, but EDI provides a useful reference point.
The Edinburgh Decentralization Index (EDI) from the University of Edinburgh is mentioned as a tool for measuring decentralization.
